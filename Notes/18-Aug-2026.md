## Drawing a Square

In this class, we began with a simple shape: a **square**. The focus was not merely on writing code that displayed the square, but on understanding the geometric logic used by the code.

To draw the square, we were given:

- Its centre point, `C = (x, y)`.
- Its side length, `l`.

Before drawing its sides, we first needed to calculate the square's **four vertices**.

---

# Finding the Four Vertices

The centre of the square is located halfway between its opposite sides. Therefore, the distance from the centre to each side is:

`l / 2`

The four vertices can be found by adding or subtracting `l / 2` from the centre's `x` and `y` coordinates in different combinations:

- `P1 = (x - l/2, y - l/2)`
- `P2 = (x + l/2, y - l/2)`
- `P3 = (x + l/2, y + l/2)`
- `P4 = (x - l/2, y + l/2)`

Each vertex uses one of the possible combinations of `+ l/2` and `- l/2` for the two coordinates.

---

# Why the Logic Works

The horizontal coordinates of the square are `x - l/2` and `x + l/2`. The distance between them is:

`(x + l/2) - (x - l/2) = l`

The same logic applies to the vertical coordinates. Therefore, the horizontal and vertical distances between adjacent vertices are both equal to `l`, ensuring that all four sides have the same length.

---

# Drawing and Joining the Sides

After calculating the four vertices, the program draws a **line between each pair of adjacent points**:

- `P1 → P2`
- `P2 → P3`
- `P3 → P4`
- `P4 → P1`

The final line joins the fourth point back to the first point, closing the shape and completing the square.
